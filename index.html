    OnGrid

    Version 2.88

    A live picture of the space around you in EVE Online: kills, traffic, ratting, intel, sov and wormholes, with alerts. It runs entirely in your browser from this one file.

    Getting started

    1. Open the page in Chrome or Edge on the computer that runs EVE.
    2. Type a system name at the top right, choose how many jumps around it to cover (1 to 5) and press Load. Or connect your chat logs folder (see Intel and location) and the page starts in the system you're in.
    3. Press + Alerts and settings, below the map and the watched systems, to choose what alerts you and how.

    A link like index.html?system=NAME&jumps=3 always opens that system. Otherwise the page starts where you are, or in the last system you loaded.

    Watched systems

    • The Watched systems panel is below the map. Type system names into its box, separated by commas, and press Watch. They can be anywhere, on the map or not.
    • Each card shows kills in the last hour and 24 hours, ship jumps and NPC kills in the last hour, the latest intel that mentions it, how far it is from you, and tags for gate camps, big ships, sov, ESS and wormholes.
    • Open map opens that system's map in a new browser tab. That tab stays on the system: it never follows you or moves to where you are, even with Follow me on. Remove stops watching it.
    • With several tabs open, each alert plays in only one of them.
    • With "Always alert on … watched systems" on, any intel, new kill, gate camp or sov fight there alerts you, however far away it is.
    • Kills in watched systems also appear in the kill feed, marked "watched".

    EVE login and notifications

    The login also tells the page which system each logged-in character is in, checked every 10 seconds, and whether they're online. This works without the chat logs folder, even on another computer. You can turn it off with "Get my location from my EVE login" in Alerts and settings. A character logged in before version 2.5 shows Allow location: press it and approve once more.

    Log in with EVE to see your characters' notifications in the Character notifications panel: structures under attack, shields and armor lost with the timer, fuel and low power warnings, sov events, wars, kill reports and the rest. Attacks put an ATTACKED tag on the system for an hour.

    On the official page, https://ongrid07.github.io/OnGrid/, all of this is already set up: just press Log in with EVE in Alerts and settings. A copy hosted anywhere else needs its own one-time setup:

    1. Put the page on a web address. EVE's login can't return to a file opened from your disk. Two ways:
      • GitHub Pages: make a repository, upload this file as index.html, then in the repository's Settings → Pages choose to deploy from the main branch. The page appears at https://YOUR-NAME.github.io/REPOSITORY/.
      • Your own computer: in the folder with the file, run python -m http.server 8000 and open http://localhost:8000/.
    2. Go to developers.eveonline.com, sign in, and create an application. Choose the type that allows authentication and API access, add the permissions the page asks for (notifications, location, online status, ship type, fatigue, waypoints, open windows, contacts for character, corp and alliance, and fleet), and set the callback URL to the address shown under EVE login in Alerts and settings (the Copy button copies it).
    3. Copy the app's Client ID into the Client ID box. The page doesn't need the secret key, so never paste the secret anywhere.
    4. Press Log in with EVE and approve. Repeat for each character you want.

    Notifications update every 5 minutes, but CCP only refreshes them about every 10, so they can be up to 10 minutes behind. Notifications that were already there when you log in don't alert. Your login is saved in this browser only. Log out removes it and cancels it with CCP.

    Layout

    From the top: the summary numbers, with Thera/Turnur connections and sov fights side by side below them (each box lights up when there's something in range, and a sov fight under way pulses red); the gate map with the systems table and intel beside it; watched systems; then Alerts and settings, Character notifications and Kills. Each of the last three has a – button that collapses it into a small button in a row above them. Press the small button to open it again. The page remembers which are open. A collapsed Character notifications button shows how many attacks arrived in the last hour.

    Travel map

    Set a destination and the Route planner shows your route as a travel map. Ways to set one: type a system in the Destination box at the top right of the Gate map and press Route (it suggests systems on the map and watched systems, but any system works); click a system on the map and press Safe route or Set destination; a timer's Destination button; or the Route planner's own form. The Route planner opens by itself. The Gate map stays as it is, with the route drawn on it, and next to the Destination box it shows → destination, jumps left and ⚠ with how many systems ahead have something reported; × ends the trip.

    In the Route planner, the trip bar shows the destination, jumps left, the next system and how many systems ahead have something reported.

    • The route is drawn step by step, in rows, start to destination. Each system shows its name and security status, colored like EVE. The dashed ring is you, the blue ring your next jump, and systems you've passed fade.
    • Above a system: ✕ kills there in the last hour (EVE's own numbers, so this works anywhere, not only on the area map), INTEL with the reported pilot count and minutes since it was reported, and CAMP for gate camps the page has spotted, with a pulsing red ring around the system. Dangerous systems glow red. Hover a system for the details.
    • It follows you: as you jump the route moves along; if you leave the route it finds a new one from where you are; when you arrive the trip ends.
    • Intel about a system still ahead on your route alerts you, however far away it is.
    • Safer route (shown when something is reported ahead) finds a route around the dangerous systems. Send to autopilot sets the destination in EVE, and the autopilot plans the way there itself. End trip clears it. The trip is remembered if you reload.
    • EVE doesn't tell other programs about a destination you set inside the game, so set it from this page to get the travel map.

    Moving and resizing boxes

    On a window at least 900 pixels wide, every box can be moved and resized straight away. Drag a box by its title to move it. Drag any edge or corner (top, bottom, left, right, or diagonally from any corner) to resize it; the pointer changes and the box gets a dashed outline when you're on an edge. Boxes snap to a small grid. The map grows and shrinks with its box.

    To reorder boxes, drag one by its title until the pointer is over another box. The other box gets a blue outline and slides into the spot you're dragging from. Let go and the two have swapped places, each taking the other's position and size. This works left and right as well as up and down.

    If you move or resize a box over others without the pointer on one, they shrink to make room, on whichever side keeps them biggest, and show an orange outline while you drag. If there's no room beside it, a box moves below instead. Drag away again before letting go and they spring back.

    Lock boxes at the top locks every box in place and size, so nothing moves by accident. Press Unlock boxes to move them again. Your layout and the lock are saved and included in Copy my settings. Standard layout puts everything back. Narrower windows and phones always use the standard layout.

    Windows app

    OnGrid for Windows is this page in its own app window. Download OnGrid-Setup-….exe from the releases page and run it; it installs in a few seconds and adds a Start menu shortcut.

    • The launcher (app 1.3 and later): the app opens to a round gate ring. Each symbol on the ring opens one box in its own window: Gate map, Wormholes and sov fights (Thera/Turnur connections and sov fights in range), Intel and location, Local and D-scan, Hostile tracker, Watched systems, Systems, Kills, Character notifications, Jabber, Loot tracker, Timers, Route planner, Alerts and settings, and About; the power symbol quits. Hover a symbol to see its name in the middle. A small blue dot under a symbol means its window is open; clicking the symbol again closes that window. Click the middle of the ring to open the full OnGrid window. Drag the ring from anywhere, even from a symbol, to move it (it remembers where you put it), and press hide (or Esc) to tuck it into the tray. Each box window remembers its size and place, and (in the app only) has ⋮ (Settings) in its title bar, next to the minimize button, that shows only the settings for that window (the Gate map's map and jump bridge settings, the Intel window's alerts, characters and phone relay, and so on); in the full window it opens Alerts and settings. When you quit the app, it remembers which windows were open (the ring, the full window and each box) and where they were, maximized or not, and puts them all back next time it starts; a window that was on a screen that's no longer connected opens on the main screen. The Gate map window shows just the map. In its title row, the small 2j-style picker changes how many jumps the map shows, and the ☰ button shows or hides System, Jumps, Load, presets and the summary above it. Every window shows the same intel, rings, tracks and alerts: the window that reads your chat logs (or the phone relay) shares them with the others. To start with the full window instead, untick Open to the launcher in the tray menu. Launcher size in the tray menu makes the ring small, medium (the usual) or large.
    • Overlays (app 1.4 and later): two see-through windows that float on top of EVE when the game runs in windowed or fixed-window mode. They are separate windows and never touch the game client. The alert banner flashes at the top of the screen whenever OnGrid alerts you (hostiles in range, watched systems, big ships, camps, sov, timers) and fades after about nine seconds; several at once show a +count. The intel HUD stays up and shows your system and its security, a danger rating (HIGH: a hostile reported within 1 jump in the last 5 minutes, or a camp next door; MEDIUM: hostiles within 3 jumps, kills where you are, camps or big ships nearby; LOW: quiet), the nearest reported hostile with jumps, pilots and minutes ago, how many systems within 3 jumps have hostiles reported, big ships, and kills where you are. The travel HUD appears only while a trip is on: destination and jumps left, then your system and the next five jumps with their security, kills in the last hour (✕), intel (with pilot count) and a pulsing ring on camps, plus a warning for the first camp ahead. The mini map is a small see-through copy of the gate map (small, medium or large), showing at most 3 jumps even when the Gate map shows more, with the intel rings, hostile tracks and your YOU ring; to stay readable it only names systems with intel, kills, you or hostile groups. The home HUD watches the home system you type under Overlays over the game and everything within 3 jumps of it, wherever you are: a danger rating, the nearest hostile reported near home, how many systems near home have hostiles, kills in your home system in the last hour, and camps. Clicks pass straight through all of them to EVE. Switch them in Alerts and settings → Overlays over the game (or the tray menu): turn each one on or off, Move overlays to drag them where you want (they get a yellow dashed outline; untick it to lock them again), Overlay background to make them lighter or more solid, and Put overlays back in place. Test alert in Alerts and settings shows the banner.
    • Jabber (app 1.8 and later): the speech-bubble symbol on the ring. Press Account and type your Jabber ID (name@server, the one your alliance or corp auth site gave you) and password, then Connect. If your auth site gives a separate server name or port, type them under Server and Port; otherwise leave them empty. The password is kept encrypted by Windows on your PC, is only sent to your Jabber server over an encrypted connection, and OnGrid won't connect to a server whose certificate isn't valid. With Connect when OnGrid starts on, it connects by itself; Disconnect keeps it off until you connect again, and Forget password removes the saved password.
      • Pings: broadcasts from your ping bot show under 📣 Pings, newest first, with an alert (a sonar ping sound, a Windows pop-up and the banner over the game, following your alert settings; ▶ Ping sound under Account plays the sound). A message counts as a ping when it's a server broadcast, comes from one of the Ping senders (directorbot, pingbot and similar names to start with; add your alliance's bot if it has another name), says "This was a broadcast from", or is said in one of your Ping rooms. Pings are kept on your PC; pings you missed while offline show without an alert. Clear empties the list. Pings never go to the phone relay.
      • Chat: rooms under Rooms to join are joined when you connect (a short name like fleet means fleet@conference.yourserver). Join another with the box under Rooms: it's added to Rooms to join, so it's joined again every time you connect, and Leave takes it off the list. Start a chat with someone under Chats (your Jabber contacts are suggested). Each room always shows its MOTD (the room's topic) at the top, with who set it; the last one is remembered, so it shows even before you connect. Enter sends, Shift+Enter starts a new line. With Alert me for direct messages… on, a direct message or your name said in a room gives a quieter alert, unless you're reading that conversation. Chat isn't saved: it starts fresh (with the room's recent messages) each time OnGrid starts.
    • Loot tracker (app 1.7 and later): the crate symbol on the ring. Press Connect game logs folder once and pick Documents\EVE\logs\Gamelogs (next to the Chatlogs folder); the app opens it by itself after that. Every bounty is counted the moment you kill the rat, without waiting for the 20-minute payout, for all your characters logged in at once. A session starts by itself with your first bounty or loot paste (or press Start); Pause stops the clock and the counting, Resume carries on, and New session clears it. For loot, select the items in your cargo hold or a wreck, press Ctrl+C, then paste them into the Loot box: they're priced at EVE's estimated value (the same as the game's Est. price) and added up; click a paste to see its items, or × to remove it. Bounties are shown after each pilot's corp tax, looked up by itself; type a number in Tax on bounties to use your own percentage instead. ESS payouts (your share of the ESS bank, paid into your wallet) are added too: they aren't in the game logs, so OnGrid reads them from the wallet of each character you're logged in with under EVE login (press Allow new features there once so it may read the wallet). CCP updates the wallet about once an hour, so they can show up to an hour late; a payout counts for the session it arrives in. ISK per hour is bounties after tax plus ESS payouts plus loot, divided by the time the session has run (pauses left out). The loot HUD over the game shows total ISK, ISK per hour, bounties and loot while a session runs; turn it on or off in Overlays over the game (the ⋮ in the Loot tracker window) or the tray menu. Past sessions: pressing New session keeps the finished session in the history. Pick two dates (or press 7 days or 30 days) to add up every session between them: total ISK, time, ISK per hour, and bounties, ESS and loot. Each day is listed with its total; click a day to see its sessions, and × deletes one. The history is in your backups.
    • It keeps running in the system tray, and alerts and Windows notifications keep working while it's minimized or behind the game. Closing the window hides it to the tray; quit from the tray icon.
    • Right-click the tray icon for Always on top, Keep running when closed and Start with Windows.
    • It remembers your chat logs folder, so there's no permission prompt each time.
    • It loads this live page, so every page update shows up in the app straight away. The app itself updates itself too: it checks for a new version as soon as it starts and every hour, and downloads it in the background. An update found while the app is starting installs straight away (OnGrid restarts in a few seconds with your windows); one found later installs by itself as soon as no OnGrid window has been in front for about a minute (for example while you're playing): OnGrid closes, updates silently and starts again in a few seconds, with the launcher and box windows you had open, without taking the focus from the game. Click the "update ready" notification, or Restart to update in the tray menu, to do it straight away. Check for updates in the tray menu, or in Alerts and settings → OnGrid for Windows, checks now and tells you what it found; that section also shows the app's version and Restart to update once an update is ready.
    • There's also a portable …-portable.exe that runs without installing. It can't replace itself, so it only tells you when a new version is out and opens the download page.
    • Your settings in the app are separate from your browser's; use Backup and other devices to copy them across.
    • It opens your chat logs folder by itself when it starts (app 1.5.3 and later), so there's no Reconnect folder to press. Windows may say "Windows protected your PC" because the app isn't signed with a paid certificate: click More info → Run anyway.

    Presets

    The Presets row under the header saves the current map (center system, jumps and watched systems) under a name. Click a preset to switch back to it; × deletes it. Loading a preset turns Follow me off so the map stays where you put it.

    Local & D-scan

    The box starts with just D-scan. Paste your D-scan first; once it's in, an + Add Local button appears to open the Local box for the Local list. Clearing both puts it back to D-scan only.

    Local: in EVE, click a name in the Local member list, press Ctrl+A then Ctrl+C, and paste into the Local box. Everyone is grouped by alliance (or corp), hostile groups first and opened, blue groups last. Pilots who aren't blue are checked on zKillboard (up to 60 per paste); anyone dangerous (danger 70% or more with 20+ kills) or flagged as a likely cloaky hunter or cyno alt is listed under Watch out. Blues and hostiles come from your EVE contacts and the Standings lists. Open a group to see each pilot, with an in EVE button for their info window.

    D-scan: run the directional scanner, click a result, press Ctrl+A then Ctrl+C, and paste into the D-scan box. You get the number of ships (and how many are on grid, within about 8,000 km), structures, deployables, drones and fighters, ships counted by class and by type, and Threats: capitals, dictors, HICs, Black Ops, bombers, recons, Strategic Cruisers, interceptors, covert ops and logistics. Hover a threat to see the nearest one's distance.

    Results show under a header with the system and how long ago you scanned, then side-by-side cards: Ships and Ship Classes from the D-scan, Alliances and Corporations from Local, each with counts, logos and tickers. Hostile groups have a red edge and blue groups a blue edge; click one to see its pilots. Watch out and Threats sit above the cards.

    Copy, in the results header, copies a message for Discord laid out like the results: the system and scan time, the summary, Watch out and Threats, then the cards as two columns of aligned text (Ships next to Ship Classes, Alliances next to Corporations) with tickers and counts. Hostile rows start with "!" and blue rows with "+". Long lists are shortened with "+N more" so it fits in one Discord message. On a phone, Discord may wrap the two columns. The System box fills in with where you are (or the map's center); type a different system to label the scan with that instead.

    Share link, next to Copy, copies a link to a page that shows only the scan: the system, when it was scanned, Watch out, Threats, the summary and all four cards, with a small link to the full tool at the top. Nothing else on the page loads for whoever opens it. Post it in Discord and anyone can click it. The link is always short (about 50 characters), so it pastes neatly into Discord or fleet chat: the scan is kept on the ntfy.sh relay under a random name for 12 hours. If the relay can't be reached, or today's free allowance is used up, the link carries the whole scan instead: long, but it works and never expires. Big fights work too: a Local with thousands of pilots is saved in several pieces that the link puts back together (about 4 pieces per 1,000 pilots, up to roughly 10,000 pilots). Each piece counts toward ntfy.sh's free 250 messages a day, shared with the phone relay. Ship names people gave their ships aren't included. Whoever opens the link sees blues and hostiles by their own standings.

    Paste a new scan any time to replace the old one; nothing is saved or sent anywhere except the look-ups to EVE and zKillboard.

    Hostile tracker

    Lists every non-friendly pilot named in intel in the last hour: corp and alliance, the systems they were reported in (oldest first), where they were last seen and how long ago, and whether they're closing in on you or moving away. Their paths are drawn on the map for 30 minutes: a faint track with dashes flowing from where they were first reported toward where they were last seen, and an arrowhead on their latest system. Tracks follow the gates: when intel skips systems, the line goes through the systems in between. If two sightings can't be joined by gates on the map, or are too many jumps apart for the time between the reports (a wormhole, a jump bridge or a wrong report), the track simply breaks there rather than drawing a line straight across.

    Each group has its own color: pilots are grouped by alliance, or by corp if they have no alliance, or on their own. The group's ticker (or name) and how many of them there are show at the end of its newest track (stacked in a column when several groups are in the same system), and pilots of one group who flew the same way share one thicker line. Tracks of different groups on the same gate are drawn slightly apart. Each pilot's row has its group's color on the left. Click a pilot to highlight just their path; click again to show all.

    Timers

    Every upcoming timer from your characters' notifications in one list, soonest first: structures leaving reinforcement, sov command nodes, moon chunks ready and breaking, and other timed events. You can add your own with Add timer: write the time as "in 2h 30m", "1d 4h" or an EVE date like 2026.11.20 18:00. Timers under an hour show red, under 6 hours orange. With "Alert 60 and 15 minutes before each timer" on, you get an alert at both points.

    Quiet hours

    Click a system on the map: under the bar above the map, a grid shows when kills usually happen there, by weekday and hour in EVE time (from zKillboard). It says whether the system is usually quiet, somewhat active or busy right now, and its quietest stretch of hours. Watched-system cards say the same for the current hour.

    Phone relay

    Phones can't read EVE's chat logs, so your PC can send its intel to your phone:

    1. On the PC, in Alerts and settings → Phone relay, tick "send my intel to my phone".
    2. On the phone, scan the QR code or open the link shown. That pairs the phone and turns receiving on; the intel then shows in the phone's intel panel, with the map rings, counts, hostile tracker and alerts.

    Messages are encrypted on the PC with a key from the pairing code and pass through ntfy.sh, a free public relay that can't read them. ntfy.sh allows 250 messages a day per internet connection, so intel is sent in bundles (every minute by default) and only for systems on the map or watched, unless you include the rest. The settings show how much of the day's allowance is used. New code makes a new pairing code; pair the phone again afterwards.

    The phone checks for new intel every 15 seconds while the page is open, and straight away when you switch back to it. Anything sent while the phone was locked is caught up then (it doesn't alert for intel that's more than 3 minutes old). The line under the intel panel says when it last checked and when intel last arrived.

    Phones pause web pages in the background. For alerts with the screen off, install the ntfy app, subscribe to the topic shown in the settings, and tick "push readable alerts". Those alerts are not encrypted, so keep the topic name private.

    Using your EVE login

    These need extra permissions. A character logged in before version 2.8 shows Allow new features under EVE login: press it and approve once. Actions use the character you're flying (your latest jump), or another logged-in character that has the permission.

    • Set destination / Add waypoint: click a system on the map (or in the systems table) and use the bar above the map, or press Destination on a watched-system card. Your autopilot in EVE updates straight away.
    • Show in EVE opens the selected system's info window in the game. The in EVE button next to a pilot named in intel opens that pilot's info window.
    • Route planner (collapsed below the watched systems, or Safe route in the map bar): from where you are (or a system you type) to a destination. It asks EVE for a route that avoids systems with gate camps, kills in the last hour or hostiles reported in intel in the last 15 minutes (each can be switched off), and compares it with the shortest route. The route is drawn on the map. Send to autopilot sets the destination in EVE, and your autopilot plans its own way there (using jump bridges if they're turned on in your autopilot settings in the game). When the route goes around danger, it also adds the fewest waypoints needed to keep the autopilot on the safer route, and says which. Only systems on the map and watched systems are checked for danger.
    • Standings from contacts: your personal, corp and alliance contacts are read every 30 minutes. Positive standing counts as friendly and negative as hostile, and your own corp and alliance are always friendly. Names typed under Standings are added on top.
    • Fleet: when a logged-in character is fleet boss, each member appears on the map as F and a count (and FLEET in the table), checked every 30 seconds. Hover the fleet line under the intel panel for the list. Other fleet members can't see positions; that's EVE's rule.
    • Ship-aware alerts: the ship you're flying shows next to your location. In a hauler, transport, freighter, mining barge, exhumer, mining frigate, Rorqual or industrial command ship, alerts reach one jump further and gate camps always alert.
    • Jump fatigue shows as FATIGUE with the time left next to your location, checked every 5 minutes and after each jump.

    Gate map

    • Systems are laid out like EVE's own star map seen from above: each one where it is in space, so what's north, south, east or west matches the game. Busy clusters are opened out a little so names don't overlap, but every system keeps its place relative to its neighbours. Region names are written faintly over their systems. Lines are stargates. To get the older layout, with systems in rings by jumps from the centre, choose Map layout: Rings by jumps in Alerts and settings.
    • Dot color: red is a kill in the last hour, orange a kill in the last 6 hours, blue is ship traffic only, grey is quiet. Bigger dots mean more ship jumps in the last hour.
    • When the map reaches into other regions, each region's systems are kept together in their own slice of the map. Other regions are shaded in a faint color with their name in it; your own region is unshaded and its name is grey. Hover a shaded area to see which region it is.
    • Jump bridges: paste your alliance's jump bridge list into Alerts and settings → Jump bridges and press Save bridges (any list with two system names per line works: tables with "@ 1-1", lines like "1053773110271 RD-G2R --> P-33KR (FADE)", or simply "A » B"; lines starting with # are skipped, and you can paste several alliances' lists at once). Bridges with both ends on the map are drawn as dashed green curves; a system whose bridge leads off the map gets a green JB tag, and hovering a system names where its bridge goes. Hostile tracks also follow bridges. In the Route planner, tick Use jump bridges to let routes take them: the route list marks each bridge with JB » system, the travel map with JB», and the note says how many it uses. EVE's autopilot takes them too when jump bridges are turned on in its settings in the game. (EVE's route service accepts 50 bridges per route, so OnGrid uses the 50 nearest the way you're going.) The list stays in this browser (it's included in Copy my settings) and is never sent anywhere.
    • Green glow is ratting: NPC kills in the last hour. The label shows rats per hour.
    • A purple ring is a system named in intel in the last 15 minutes. It pulses for the first 3 minutes. A number in brackets is the reported pilot count. When every pilot named in a system's latest report has since been sighted in another system, that ring goes away and the systems table shows "moved", so the rings follow the hostiles. Reports that don't name pilots (like "5 nv") keep their ring for the full 15 minutes.
    • A dashed ring and YOU mark where your character is.
    • Sov fights come from two places. CCP's public list only shows a fight once a structure has been reinforced. Your characters' notifications fill the gap: ENTOSIS (someone started entosising, shown for 90 minutes), REINFORCED (with the time the command nodes appear), NODES (command nodes are up) and LOST. These show in the Sov fights box even for systems off the map, marked "notification".
    • Labels: CAMP possible gate camp, big ship type (e.g. DICTOR), VULN sov hub vulnerable, FIGHT sov fight, ESS ESS activity reported, ATTACKED a structure attack from your notifications, TH/TU Thera or Turnur wormhole.
    • Click a system to show only its kills in the kill feed, and a bar above the map with Set destination, Add waypoint, Safe route, Show in EVE and Open in new tab. Click it again to clear.
    • A thick blue line is the route from the Route planner. F and a number is how many of your fleet are there.

    Intel and location

    • EVE writes each chat channel to a file on your computer. In EVE's settings, turn on Log Chat to File.
    • Type your intel channel's name exactly as it appears on its tab (several, separated by commas), then click Connect chat logs folder and pick Documents\EVE\logs\Chatlogs. Next time, press Reconnect folder.
    • The page reads new messages every 1.5 seconds. It recognises system names, and shortened names (the first few characters) when only one system matches.
    • Pilot counts: "+5", "5x", "5 nv", "5 reds", "local 12" and "x5". "clr" or "clear" sets the count to 0. A count only applies when a message names one system.
    • Pilot names in intel are looked up: corp, alliance, zKillboard danger and solo %, kills, usual ships. "CLOAKY HUNTER?" and "CYNO ALT?" are guesses from their history. EVE has characters named after almost every common word, so a name only counts as a pilot when it has zKillboard history or is two or more words; a brand-new single-word character with no kills or losses won't be picked up.
    • Big-ship words (titan, super, dread, carrier, fax, lancer, rorq, dictor, hic, blops, caps, cyno) and ship names like "Sabre" get tagged.
    • ESS words (ess, linking, main bank, reserve bank, bank) get an ESS tag.
    • Show lists all intel, or only messages about systems on the map or watched.
    • Above the intel, each of your characters is listed with where it is. Choose which ones are listed under Characters in Intel and location in Alerts and settings: click a name to hide it (it's crossed out) and again to show it. This only changes the list; hidden characters still count for your location, alerts and the YOU ring on the map. The choice is saved. With alts logged in, also pick your Main character there: only that character's location moves the map and counts for distances, alerts and "closing in" (Automatic follows whoever moved last). Several alts in the same intel channel each log the same lines; OnGrid reads one copy and shows each report once.
    • Your location comes from your EVE login (see below) or from your Local chat log, whichever saw your latest jump. Each source is named next to your system. With several characters, each one is shown. A character the EVE login reports as offline isn't shown.

    Kill feed

    • The last 24 hours, newest first, up to 150 kills, with ship, system, gate, victim, attackers and value. Each kill links to zKillboard.
    • Tags: LOSS a friendly died, KILL friendlies got the kill, HOSTILE DOWN a listed hostile died, STRUCTURE a structure died, and big ship types.

    Alerts and settings

    • Alert on intel within N jumps, counted from where you are or from the map's center.
    • Alert as a sound, a desktop pop-up, spoken words, or any mix. The browser tab also flashes when the page is in the background. Test alert shows what you'll get.
    • Optional alerts: new kills and gate camps in range; big ships anywhere on the map; sov fights and structure kills anywhere on the map, and ESS reports in range; anything in watched systems.
    • Follow me re-centers the map each time you jump.
    • Kills to load per system: 200, 500, 1,000 (default) or 2,000, covering up to 24 hours. Higher limits download more in busy systems.
    • Switches for ratting glow, sov and ESS, and Thera and Turnur wormholes.
    • Standings: full alliance or corp names (not tickers) for friendly and hostile. You can hide friendly losses from the kill feed.

    Everything you set is saved in this browser straight away, including text as you type it (the note at the top of Alerts and settings flashes "Saved"). The page asks the browser to keep this data rather than clearing it to free space. Settings are per browser and per device: use Backup and other devices → Copy my settings, then paste them into the same box on the other device and press Load pasted settings. This copies settings, presets, your own timers, intel channels and the section layout, but never your EVE login. On iPhone, a page added to the Home Screen keeps its own settings separate from Safari.

    How often it updates

    Intel, and your location from the chat logevery 1.5 seconds
    Intel sent to your phonein bundles every 30 seconds to 5 minutes (your choice)
    Your location from the EVE loginevery 10 seconds (CCP updates about every 5); online status every minute
    Kill feed and kill countsevery 2 minutes
    Hourly stats, sov, wormholesevery 10 minutes
    New log files (after relogging)every 30 seconds
    Character notificationsevery 5 minutes (CCP refreshes about every 10)

    Refresh at the top updates kills, hourly stats and notifications right away.

    What it can't show

    • Local member counts, anomalies and signatures, ESS bank values, other people's structure timers and jump bridges. None of these are published by CCP. Your own structure timers appear only when a notification includes them.
    • Chrome may block desktop pop-ups for a file opened from your disk. Hosting the page on a website fixes that.
